\section{Purpose}
%====================
The purpose of this brief is to articulate a product strategy for the 
Computational Injury Analysis Group within Terminal Ballistic Department 
(Department 5421) at Sandia National Laboratories.                                                                              

This brief is in response to Wallace 
Bow,\footnote{See email 2016-10-31 1433 MST.}
which stated that the Center (Integrated Military Systems, Center 5400)

prioritize our most pressing technological issues to guarantee that our 
innovation investments are being properly allocated.''  The end goal of 
effort and funding allocation is ``to ensure that our products 
remain superior.''

\section{Mission and Vision}
%====================
\subsection{In a nutshell}
\begin{itemize}
\item The Laboratory's mission focuses on the solution of complex national
security problems with nuclear deterrence and the derivatives therefrom, 
with the vision of solving these problems into the 21st century.
\item The DSA mission focuses on national security solutions by 
leverage of research and development that is synergistic to the 
nuclear deterrent. 
The vision articulates warfighter and intelligence community superiority 
from DSA's transformation of research into solutions. 
\item The IMS mission focuses on national security through solution of
warfighters' problems and visualizes that the result will be game-changing.
\item The SAS mission focuses on the launch, fly, navigate, and penetrate
of high speed weapon systems.
\item The TBT mission focuses on survivability and functionality of 
ballistics technology.  
\item The MST mission focuses on enhanced military operations and mission
effectiveness.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Connection to the customer}
The national security mission is supported at all levels of the 
organization.  The execution of that mission can be broadly  
subdivided into military and intelligence customers.  Service of 
the military customer can be further divided into 
area of application: air, land, and sea (e.g., Air Force, Army, and
Navy).  Cross-cutting these application environments, 
service to national security can be 
categorized into human-exposed and human-non-exposed applications.  
With the human-exposed category, the 
Computational Injury Analysis Group focuses on the two most 
important exposure areas:  blast and ballistics.  

\subsection{Computational Injury Analysis Group mission and vision}
With this context
of the organizational missions and our connection to the organization's
structure, we now state our mission and vision.

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent {\bf Mission:  We analyze injury from blast and ballistics 
to help the nation protect the U.S. warfighter.} 

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent {\bf Vision: To significantly reduce the risk of death or serious injury from 
blast and ballistics by 2027.}

\subsection{Exploration of word choice}
To better understand the mission and vision statements, let us now 
weigh out each word and explore not only the denotation, but also the
connotation.

{\bf Why computational?}  Because the core competency of our group is 
within modeling and simulation.  We, by design, exclude 
experimental work from efforts we undertake.  Our interaction with 
experimental groups, therefore, is collaborative but not organizational.

{\bf Why injury?}  Because injury, or specifically, the reduction or 
elimination of injury, is fundamental to the nation's ability to marshal 
national security resources that require a human in-the-loop.   We emphasize 
the word ``injury'' because the analysis goes beyond demonstration of 
engineering response (e.g., stress, strain) of human tissue and human 
personal protection (e.g., bullet proof vest) to war environments.  
The analysis connects engineering response to human threshold of injury 
to provide evidence-based prediction on injury severity: minor, moderate, 
serious, severe (survivability likely) critical (survivability unknown), 
and maximum (not 
survivable).\footnote{See the Abbreviated Injury Scale (AIS), 
by the Association for the Advancement of Automotive Medicine (AAAM).}

{\bf Why analysis?}  Because our main product is the advancement of science 
to understand injury mechanisms.  There is an implicit priority of 
analysis first, development second.  Specifically, our primary focus will be 
to use {\em existing} simulation tools (e.g., ALEGRA, CTH, Sierra) and
{\em existing} models (e.g., BOB, Global Human Body Models 
Consortium,\footnote{See https://youtu.be/u8l9I\_k1jCU} and 
Total Human Model for Safety\footnote{See www.lstc.com/thums}) to
provide insight and answers to scientific problems and questions.
Fundamentally, our focus with tools and models will be as a means 
to an end, and not as the end itself. 

{\bf Why blast and ballistics?}  Because the 
exposure of the warfighter to bombs and high velocity munitions continue 
to be a specific and relevant risk to the U.S. warfighter.  We emphasize 
that blast and ballistics is distinct from general impact because the 
former has characteristically large forces and velocities that occur  
in extremely small times.  
This confluence of scales creates an  
environment that is extremely hostile to human viability.  

{\bf Why by 2027?}  Because we believe pursuit of significant reduction to 
the risk of death or serious injury to the U.S. warfighter will remain an 
enduring research endeavor over the next decade, worthy of considerable 
investments of time and resources.  It is interesting to conceive of 
autonomous systems (e.g., robots) taking the place of a human soldier on 
the war field.  In this scenario, our mission may evolve, but our vision 
would remain unchanged.  Our mission, therefore, might shift to protection 
of the human surrogate from malfunction or obliteration secondary to exposure 
to blast or ballistics.  In this way, we have attempted to be forward-thinking 
in our vision, while calibrating a mission to important, present-day needs.

\section{Strategy}
%====================
\subsection{Motivation}
In a global context, we direct our efforts to protect people who promote 
peace.  The specific implementation of this ideal is through the 
U.S. warfighter, called upon by service to country to fight tyranny and 
the abuse of power.  ``Life, liberty and the pursuit of all who threaten it,''
the U.S. Navy's slogan, adroitly encapsulates the warfighter's mission.
To that end, it is incumbent on those who benefit from a warfighter's risk 
taking that we help to minimize exposure to that risk.  Specifically, as long 
as blast and ballistics remain an inherent element of conflict, we 
should equip the warfighter with the best available protection from the same.  

Fundamental to the best available protection paradigm is the understanding 
of how blast and ballistics imperil the warfighter's survivability by 
exposing the warfighter to traumatic brain injuries that are either focal 
(penetrating through the warfighter's helmet or face, impact to a side of 
the helmet or face) or diffuse (causing localized shear damage to the 
neural microstructure of the brain).  

\subsection{Background (continued from Section~\ref{section:background})}
Our strategy is build from our extraordinary success since our first 
funding in FY2007.  However, before we chart the path forward, it is 
important to lay out, in an abbreviated form, the foundation from which we 
build and articulate how our group is unique in the world and thus 
positioned to offer the nation a significant value proposition.  

{\bf What is our current product?}  
\begin{itemize}
\item We have developed 
and validated human tissue constitutive models for use in Sandia's Sierra 
and CTH computational code bases.  {\em [Need to be specific as to what
models, and reconcile previous emphasis on tool use, not tool building 
(analyst role versus developer role).]}
\item We have developed and validated a human 
anatomical model of the head, neck, and torso, having 1~millimeter resolution, 
based on the Visible Human Project.  {\em [Again, need for reconciliation of
strategy: tool use versus tool development.]}
\item We have postulated to the 
scientific community, through peer-reviewed journal publications and
scholarly conferences, 
that critical thresholds of deviatoric strain 
energy in the human brain secondary to blast correlate with traumatic brain 
injury (TBI) and clinical functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) data.
Moreover, we have suggested that the brain has a compensatory mechanism
ability, where a non-injured part of the brain will attempt an 
uptake in function to compensate for the damaged areas of the brain.  
This compensatory strategy is well-known in human ambulation and 
gait mechanics, but remains relatively nascent in brain science.
\item Finally, we have demonstrated that the presence of air bubbles
in cerebral spinal fluid (CSF) after blast to the brain result in 
cavitation, which is a damage mechanism to the myelin sheath, the protective
covering of nerve fibers.
\item {\em Include Candice's viscous criteria effort once Doug and Paul
have cleared Chad to know details about it.}
\end{itemize}

{\bf Why have we focused on the head, neck, and torso?}  Because our strategy 
focuses our limited resources on serious-to-fatal injuries.  While fatal 
injuries can still occur secondary to injury to the upper or lower 
extremities, the prevalence in these body regions is relatively low 
compared to injuries to the head, neck, and torso.

There will be four main thrusts to the FY2017 plan, corresponding to
each of the four members of the group led by Dederman.

motivated by his anticipated retirement and the group's desire to
minimize the ``brain drain'' caused by his departure.  Specific 
deliverables are
\begin{enumerate}
\item Complete funded research obligations, including presentations and
scholarly journal articles.
\item Complete and convey documentation of human tissue model formulation and 
implementation.
\item Mentor Cooper/Haniff/Hovey with the goal of making them 
self-sufficient members of the research team:
	\begin{enumerate}
	\item Establish professional connections from Taylor to 
	balance of group.
	\item Coach proficiency with tool use (e.g., CTH) in all members.
	\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{Torso Mechanics} Candice Cooper will lead efforts to
\begin{enumerate}
\item Verify and validate human torso model with known cadaver
impact studies. Demonstrate the human torso model can reproduce known
viscous criteria benchmarks.
\item Work with a personal protection customer to solve one significant
torso injury problem caused by blast or ballistics.
\item Publish in scholarly journals
	\begin{enumerate}
	\item The validation of the torso model.
	\item The solution to an industry problem.
	\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Brain Micro-Mechanics} Shivonne Haniff will lead efforts to
\begin{enumerate}
\item Compare and contrast three micro-mechanical brain injury mechanisms: 
	\begin{enumerate}
	\item Neuron damage from cavitation
	\item Neuron damage macro-level head rotations in sagittal, frontal, 
	and transverse plane motions.\footnote{The sagittal plane rotation
	is the ``yes'' rotation, with chin up-and-down movement.  
	The frontal plane is the ``shoulder-to-shoulder'' rotation, with 
	alternating ear-to-shoulder movements.  The transverse plane 
	rotation is the ``no'' rotation, with chin alternating left-to-right
	movements.}
	\end{enumerate}
\item Harmonize macro and phenomenological brain injury models
(e.g., G and T) with micro-mechanical brain injury models.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Head-Neck Interface, Group Development, Business Development}
Chad Hovey will lead efforts to
\begin{enumerate}
	\item Atlanto-occipital dislocation (AOD) 
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item Large-slip contact of occipital-C1 junction
		\item Support for gross head motion for Haniff's work
		\item Pilot ejection head and neck injury mitigation
	\end{enumerate}
	\item Develop group's identity and purpose of 
	computational injury analysis from blast and ballistics.
	\item Develop economies of scale within group, so that 
	research efforts multiply, not just add.
	\item With Doug Dederman, develop a sustaining customer base.
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{STIPL proposed paragraph}
We will strengthen our contribution to warfighter protection from
blast and ballistics by leveraging our existing modeling and simulation
investments to pursue deeper understanding of three scenarios that
threaten warfighter survivability: (1) traumatic brain injury from blast
that causes mircoscale neuron damage from a cavitation mechanism;
(2) traumatic brain injury from ballistic aircraft ejection that causes
diffuse axonal injury from a high rotation rate and acceleration mechanism;
and (3) traumatic aortic disruption from behind armor blast and 
ballistic impact.  In patients with multi-trauma, head injury and aortic
disruption are the leading and second most common risk of death.  
Thus, in our vision to significantly reduce the risk of death and serious
injury to the warfighter from blast and ballistics by 2027, we position
ourselves to produce maximum scientific impact by addressing the two
leading causes of traumatic fatality.
