\section{Portfolio and Assets}

Let a portfolio $P$ be a collection of $n$ individual assets 
with present values $a_1, a_2, \ldots a_n$.
Assets have units of 
U.S.~dollars (USD).\footnote{When the asset is stock, bond, or 
other instrument that consists of 
fractional ownership (shares) denominated by price per share, 
then the present value of the asset is simply the number of shares
held (holdings) in the portfolio multiplied by the price per share of that
holding.}

The present valuation of the portfolio, $PV(P)$, is given as
\be
 PV(P) = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i.
\ee

\section{Portfolio Weighting}

The relative weight $w_i$ of each asset $a_i$ is simply the fraction 
of the asset's present value to the portfolio's present value, thus
\be
 w_i = \frac{a_i}{PV} = \frac{a_i}{\sum_{i=1}^n a_i}.
\ee
The sum of all weights, must equal one (1).
\be
 \sum_{i=1}^n w_i = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{a_i}{PV} = \frac{1}{PV} \sum_{i=1}^n a_i 
 = \frac{PV}{PV} = 1.
\ee

\section{Portfolio and Holdings}
Let a portfolio $P$ be a collection of assets $a_1 \ldots a_n$.
Let $h_i$ be the holding of each asset $a_i$.  The units of each holding is 
number of shares.  The units of each asset $a_i$, in present value, is dollars per
share.  Thus, the present value of each holding is $h_i a_i$.  

For a portfolio composed of two or more holdings, the present value of the 
total portfolio $PV(P)$ is given as the inner product of the vector of holdings
with the vector of assets, 

\be
 PV(P) = \vh \cdot \va.
\ee

\section{Weighting}

Let each individual asset $a_i$ be weighted by $w_i$, which is the weight relative to the sum of all assets $[a_1, a_n]$.

\begin{itemize}
 \item Each relative weight $w_i$ ("weight") is a finite positive real number $\realplus$, such that $0 \leq w_i < +\infty$, with units of number of shares per number of shares (shares of an individual asset per shares of total assets).
 \item The sum of all weights be me identically one (1).
 \begin{equation}
 \sum_{i=1}^n w_i = 1.
 \end{equation}
\end{itemize}
