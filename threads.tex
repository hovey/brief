\section{Background}
%====================

Vigil
{\em et al.}\footnote{Vigil MS, Brake MRW, VanGoethem DJ (2015) {\em 
Analysis of Modeling Parameters of Threaded Screws}, Sandia Report SAND2015-5135, 
June.} 
modeled a threaded screw as a three-dimensional wedge domain composed of a screw
and substrate (see Figure~\ref{fig:vigil_domain}).  
The Allow A-286 screw had material
properties\footnote{Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, yield stress, and 
ultimate tensile strength (respectively).}
$E=29$e+6~psi, $\nu=0.28$, $\sigma_Y = 120$e+3~psi, and
$\sigma_U = 160$e+3~psi.   The 304L stainless steel substrate had material
properties $E=28$e+6~psi, $\nu=0.28$, $\sigma_Y = 32.7$e+3~psi, and 
$\sigma_U = 78$e+3~psi.

\begin{figure}                                                                 
  \label{fig:vigil_domain}
  \begin{center}                                                                             
    \includegraphics[width=3.5in]{figs/screw_substrate.png}                 
  \end{center}                                                                   
  \caption{Reproduction of Vigil, Figure 3.}
\end{figure}  

Homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions were applied to the longitudinal
faces as required to allow the substrate to remain stationary while the 
screw to move axially.  An inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition 
was imposed on the top face screw, from zero to 0.635~mm (0.025 inches), 
shown (at maximum displacement) in Figure~\ref{fig:vigil_displaced}.

\begin{figure}                                                                 
  \label{fig:vigil_displaced}
  \begin{center}                                                                             
    \includegraphics[width=3.5in]{figs/screw_displaced.png}                 
  \end{center}                                                                   
  \caption{Reproduction of Vigil, Figure 5.}
\end{figure}  


Castelluccio 
{\em et al.}\footnote{Castelluccio GM, Brake MRW (2017 in press) {\em On the
Origin of Computational Model Sensitivity, Error, and Uncertainty in 
Threaded Fasteners}, {\em submitted to} Computers \& Structures, Second 
Preprint December 9, 2016.} examined model parameters of
geometry (shape, size, tolerance), materials (anisotropy, inhomogeneity), 
mechanics (interface friction, temperature, loading) and 
methodology (discretization errors, coding implementation errors) 
to analyze sensitivity, error, and uncertainty on the 
force-displacement response of threaded fasteners.

They considered the \#0-40~UNF bolt (see Figure~\ref{fig:UNF_bolt}) for
the simulations.
\begin{figure}                                                                 
  \label{fig:UNF_bolt}
  \begin{center}                                                                             
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/UNF_bolt}
  \end{center}                                                                   
  \caption{Reproduction of Castelluccio, Figure 2, showing the 
  anatomy and geometry of the \#-40~UNF bolt (IS units).}
\end{figure}  


Castelluccio performed a systematic comparison of 1D, 2.5D and 3D
finite element models (see Figure~\ref{fig:thread_models}).
\begin{figure}                                                                 
  \label{fig:thread_models}
  \begin{center}                                                                             
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/thread_models.png}
  \end{center}                                                                   
  \caption{Reproduction of Castelluccio, Figure 4, showing the 
  1D, 2.5D, and 3D models.} 
\end{figure}

\item BRAKE, Matthew email 2016-01-19 1439: 
\begin{itemize}
\item cc: Castelluccio, Gustavo M (gmcaste@sandia.gov); Smith, Jeffrey 
(jasmith@sandia.gov); Robertson, Brett Anthony (barober@sandia.gov)
\item ``There are several efforts that have been combined this FY by my
understanding. My former postdoc, Gustavo Castelluccio, is now working with
Jeff Smith (in 1550) on the sequel to this and other projects. Gustavo
recently finished a paper on our efforts for modeling a fastener in SIERRA,
and the conclusions are that the material model is more important than the
geometric model. i.e. if you were to use a model of a square peg instead of
the fastener, but with the right material model (i.e. elastic plastic), that
will give you a surprisingly close answer to the truth data. If, on the
other hand, you use a very detailed geometry with a poor material model
(i.e. elastic for something that will experience yielding), then you will
have a very poor answer compared to the truth data. The paper is either in
the third round of reviews or it's been accepted for publication, I'm not
sure which.
\item We're also working on spinning up a few complimentary programs here at Rice,
but I've not yet gotten a computer setup with SIERRA installed on it.
\item Another branch of research that my students and I worked on while I was
still at Sandia was looking at developing a surrogate model for the fastener
so that you could have a high fidelity characterization of the fastener's
response built into a single degree of freedom constitutive model that was
directly inserted into an FEA model (Brett Robertson did that work for Jeff
Smith and me). That showed some good agreement with modeling experiments,
and would be an area that I'd hope to find funding to continue one day.
\item I hope that helps. Let me know if you've any other questions.''
\end{itemize}
