\section{Introduction}
%====================
\subsection{Observations}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Yaw data is often suspect.
 \item Differentiation often creates a noisy result, e.g., differentiate position to get velocity, in the 
 computation of ejection speed, results in noisy ``spray'' either to the left or right (latitude) of the
 roll path (longitude).
 \item Have experimented with shutting off (zero-out) yaw rates, but retain yaw angles, and has 
 more smooth and (perhaps) more believable results.
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{Questions}
Given a canonical rollover example, e.g., Exponent Expedition dolly rollover test:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item What is the breakdown of ejection speed into components (directions: latitude and longitude) and
 into contribution source (e.g., $\omega \times r$, carrier frame speed, relative speed in rotating frame).
 \item Is there a contribution source that is dominant for all time?
 \item What is the {\em relative} contribution of each (e.g., normalize to 1.0, examine breakdown).
 \item Is there a justification for shutting off yaw rate?
 \item Is there a justification for modeling yaw rate as some smooth ``canned'' functional form, assumed 
 {\em a priori}?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Reference Frames}
Fig.~\ref{fig:referenceFrames} illustrates the reference frames.  
Let the inertial reference frame $\cF$ have unit vectors 
$[\vefa, \vefb, \vefc]$ at origin point $F$.
Unit vector $\vefa$ points to the right of the page.
Unit vector $\vefb$ points the top of the page.
Unit vector $\vefc$ points out of the page, and is perpendicular to a
level plane of vehicle travel.
These unit vectors correspond to the easting, northing, and elevation
vectors in a plan view survey coordinate system, respectively.
All unit vectors used in this paper are dextral, orthonormal, ordered triads.
We note that our coordinate system
uses the ``z'' direction as up.  This is different from the SAE
convention, which uses the ``z'' direction as down.

Let the carrier frame $\cA$ be attached to the vehicle's center of mass at point $V$.
The carrier frame has unit vectors $[\veaa, \veab, \veac]$.
Unit vector $\veaa$ points toward the front of the vehicle.
Unit vector $\veab$ points to the left of the vehicle, with the vehicle in a non-rolling configuration.
Unit vector $\veac$ points to the roof of the vehicle, with the vehicle in a non-rolling configuration.
The carrier frame translates with the vehicle frame, but is not attached to the 
vehicle. Therefore, the carrier frame does not rotate with
roll $\theta$ of the vehicle with respect to the with the vehicle roll 
axis the $\veba$ (equivalently $\veaa$).
The carrier frame rotates with the yaw $\psi$ of the vehicle with respect to the inertial frame along the vertical axis $\vefc$ (equivalently $\veac$).

Let the vehicle frame $\cB$ be fixed to the vehicle's center of mass at point $V$.
The vehicle frame has unit vectors $[\veba, \vebb, \vebc]$.
Unit vector $\veba$ points to the front of the vehicle.
Unit vector $\vebb$ points to the left of the vehicle.
Unit vector $\vebc$ points to the roof of the vehicle.

\subsection{Occupant Description at Ejection}
In our previous work (Hovey 2008), we described the position vector of the 
occupant at ejection $\vr^{FO} \in \real^3$ as
\be
 \vr^{FO} = \vr^{FV} + \vr^{VO}, 
\ee
\be
 \vr^{FO} = x \; \vefa + y \; \vefb + z \; \vefc + \chi \; \veba + \rho \cos \phi 
 \; \vebb + \rho \sin \phi \; \vebc.
\ee
We described the velocity of an occupant 
$\hframe{F}\vv^{O} \in \real^3$
ejected from a rollover vehicle with non-trivial yaw effects as
\be
\hframe{F}\vv^{O} =  \hframe{F}\vv^{V} +  \hframe{B}\vv^{O} +  \hframe{F}\vomega^{\cB} \times \vr^{VO},
 \label{eq:velocity}
\ee
where $\hframe{F}\vv^{V}$ is the velocity of the vehicle relative to the inertial frame, 
$\hframe{B}\vv^{O}$ is the velocity of the occupant relative to the vehicle frame,
$\hframe{F}\vomega^{\cB}$ is the angular velocity of the vehicle in the inertial frame,
and
$\vr^{VO}$ is the position vector from the vehicle at point $V$ to the occupant at point $O$.


Writing this equation out explicitly in terms of velocity of the vehicle center
of mass in the inertial reference frame ($\dot{x}, \dot{y}, \dot{z}$), 
the radial and longitudinal position ($\rho, \chi$) and velocity 
($\dot{\rho}, \dot{\chi}$)
of the the occupant in the vehicle reference frame,
the yaw and roll position ($\psi, \theta$) and rates ($\dot{\psi}, \dot{\theta}$), 
and using the shorthand notation of 
$\cos \alpha = \hcos \alpha, \sin \alpha = \hsin \alpha$ 
for all angle variables $\alpha$ gives
\be
\hframe{F}\vv^{O} 
= 
\left\{  
	\begin{tabular}{c}
 	  	$\dot{x}$ \\ 
 		$\dot{y}$ \\ 
 		$\dot{z}$
 	\end{tabular}
\right\}
+
\left[
  \begin{tabular}{ccc}
  $\hcos \psi$  	& $-\hsin \psi \; \hcos \theta$ & $ \hsin \psi \; \hsin \theta$ \\ 
  $\hsin \psi$   & $ \hcos \psi \; \hcos \theta$ & $-\hcos \psi \; \hsin \theta$ \\
  $0$           & $ \hsin \theta$           & $\hcos \theta$  
  \end{tabular}
\right]
\left\{  
	\begin{tabular}{c}
 	  	$\dot{\chi}$ \\ 
 		$\dot{\rho} \; \hcos \phi$ \\ 
 		$\dot{\rho} \; \hsin \phi$
 	\end{tabular}
\right\}
+
\nonumber
\ee
\be
\left[
  \begin{tabular}{ccc}
  $0$  & $-\dot{\psi}$ & $\hsin \psi \; \dot{\theta}$ \\ 
  $\dot{\psi}$   & $0$ & $-\hcos \psi \; \dot{\theta}$ \\
  $-\hsin \psi \; \dot{\theta}$  & $\hcos \psi \; \dot{\theta}$ & $0$  
  \end{tabular}
\right]
\left[
  \begin{tabular}{ccc}
  $\hcos \psi$  	& $-\hsin \psi \; \hcos \theta$ & $ \hsin \psi \; \hsin \theta$ \\ 
  $\hsin \psi$   & $ \hcos \psi \; \hcos \theta$ & $-\hcos \psi \; \hsin \theta$ \\
  $0$           & $ \hsin \theta$           & $\hcos \theta$  
  \end{tabular}
\right]
\left\{  
	\begin{tabular}{c}
 	  	$\chi$ \\ 
 		$\rho \; \hcos \phi$ \\ 
 		$\rho \; \hsin \phi$
 	\end{tabular}
\right\}
\label{eq:velocityExpanded}
\ee
which is equivalent to the result we recorded previously (Hovey 2008).

\subsection{Specialization to Roll Dominant Condition}
Suppose now that the vehicle is predominantly in a roll, with trivial yaw.
If we approximate this condition as the constant $\psi = -90^{\circ} + \epsilon$, 
where $\epsilon \approx 0$.
This means  $\dot{\psi} \neq 0$ for all time.  We are then describing 
a vehicle that has yawed approximately one quarter of a revolution clockwise, 
when viewed from above, as it moves.
Eq.~(\ref{eq:velocityExpanded}) then simplifies to 
\begin{eqnarray}
\left.\hframe{F}\vv^{O}\right|_{\psi=-90^{\circ}}
&= 
\left\{  
	\begin{tabular}{c}
 	  	$\dot{x}$ \\ 
 		$\dot{y}$ \\ 
 		$\dot{z}$
 	\end{tabular}
\right\}
+
\left[
  \begin{tabular}{ccc}
  $0$  	& $\hcos \theta$ & $-\hsin \theta$ \\ 
  $-1$   & $0$ & $0$ \\
  $0$   & $ \hsin \theta$ & $\hcos \theta$  
  \end{tabular}
\right]
\left\{  
	\begin{tabular}{c}
 	  	$\dot{\chi}$ \\ 
 		$\dot{\rho} \; \hcos \phi$ \\ 
 		$\dot{\rho} \; \hsin \phi$
 	\end{tabular}
\right\}
+
%\nonumber
%\ee
%\be
\left[
  \begin{tabular}{ccc}
  $0$  & $-\dot{\psi}$ & $\dot{\theta}$ \\ 
  $\dot{\psi}$   & $0$ & $0$ \\
  $-\dot{\theta}$  & $0$ & $0$  
  \end{tabular}
\right]
\left[
  \begin{tabular}{ccc}
  $0$  	& $\hcos \theta$ & $-\hsin \theta$ \\ 
  $-1$   & $0$ & $0$ \\
  $0$   & $ \hsin \theta$ & $\hcos \theta$  
  \end{tabular}
\right]
\left\{  
	\begin{tabular}{c}
 	  	$\chi$ \\ 
 		$\rho \; \hcos \phi$ \\ 
 		$\rho \; \hsin \phi$
 	\end{tabular}
\right\}
\label{eq:velocitySpecialCase}
%\ee
\\
%\be
&= 
\left\{  
	\begin{tabular}{c}
 	  	$\dot{x}$ \\ 
 		$\dot{y}$ \\ 
 		$\dot{z}$
 	\end{tabular}
\right\}
+
\left\{  
	\begin{tabular}{c}
 	  	$\dot{\rho} \; (\hcos \theta \; \hcos \phi - \hsin \theta \; \hsin \phi)$ \\ 
 		$-\dot{\chi}$ \\ 
 		$\dot{\rho} \; (\hsin \theta \; \hcos \phi + \hcos \theta \; \hsin \phi)$
 	\end{tabular}
\right\}
+
\left\{  
	\begin{tabular}{c}
 	  	$\rho (\hsin \theta \; \hcos \phi + \hcos \theta \; \hsin \phi) \; 
			\dot{\theta} + \chi \; \dot{\psi}$ \\ 
 		$\rho (\hcos \theta \; \hcos \phi - \hsin \theta \; \hsin \phi) \; 
			\dot{\psi} \;\;\;\;\;\;\;\; \;$ \\ 
 		$\rho (\hcos \theta \; \hcos \phi - \hsin \theta \; \hsin \phi) \; 
			\dot{\theta} \;\;\;\;\;\;\;\; \;$
 	\end{tabular}
\right\}
%\ee
\end{eqnarray}
The only contributions to the velocity 
in the $\hframe{F}\vv^{O} \cdot \vefb$ direction 
arise from vehicle speed $\dot{y}$,
occupant longitudinal speed relative to the vehicle frame $\dot{\chi}$,
and yaw rate $\dot{\psi}$.
These contributions are linear. 
Perturbations in
$\dot{y}$ alone cause one-to-one corresponding perturbations in the 
occupant's ejection speed in the $\vefb$ direction.  Identical arguments
can be made for $\dot{\chi}$ and $\dot{\psi}$.  
The significance of this development is to 
illustrate sensitivity of the occupant's $\vefb$ ejection speed.
If these values, $\dot{y}$, $\dot{\chi}$, and $\dot{\psi}$ are zero, the values for 
$\hframe{F}\vv^{O}$ are forever confined to the plane spanned by 
$\left[ \vefa, \vefc \right]$.

Next, we specialize the foregoing result for trailing and leading occupants.
To facilitate insight without stretching the model too far from 
reality, we take 
the leading occupant ejection angle 
$\phi_{\mbox{\small lead}} = 0^{\circ}$
and 
the trailing occupant ejection angle 
$\phi_{\mbox{\small trail}} = 180^{\circ}$.
Typically, when $\psi = -90^{\circ}$, 
we take 
$\phi_{\mbox{\small lead}} = 50^{\circ}$ and 
$\phi_{\mbox{\small trail}} = 145^{\circ}$.
Also, for sunroof ejection portals, we typically start
$\phi_{\mbox{\small roof}} = 90^{\circ}$.  
Thus, by taking a wide range $[0, 180]^{\circ}$, we are 
bounding the ejection parameter for $\phi$ 
below by $0^{\circ}$
and
above by $180^{\circ}$. 
Substitution of numeric values of $\phi$ produces the following two
results:
\be
\left.\hframe{F}\vv^{O}\right|^{\psi=-90^{\circ}}_{\phi=0^{\circ}} 
= 
\left\{  
	\begin{tabular}{c}
 	  	$\dot{x}$ \\ 
 		$\dot{y}$ \\ 
 		$\dot{z}$
 	\end{tabular}
\right\}
+
\left\{  
	\begin{tabular}{c}
 	  	$\dot{\rho} \; \hcos \theta$ \\ 
 		$-\dot{\chi}$ \\ 
 		$\dot{\rho} \; \hsin \theta$
 	\end{tabular}
\right\}
+
\left\{  
	\begin{tabular}{c}
 	  	$\rho \hsin \theta \; 
			\dot{\theta} + \chi \; \dot{\psi}$ \\ 
 		$\rho \hcos \theta \; 
			\dot{\psi} \;\;\;\;\;\;\;\; \;$ \\ 
 		$\rho \hcos \theta \; \; 
			\dot{\theta} \;\;\;\;\;\;\;\; \;$
 	\end{tabular}
\right\}
\ee
\be
\left.\hframe{F}\vv^{O}\right|^{\psi=-90^{\circ}}_{\phi=180^{\circ}} 
= 
\left\{  
	\begin{tabular}{c}
 	  	$\dot{x}$ \\ 
 		$\dot{y}$ \\ 
 		$\dot{z}$
 	\end{tabular}
\right\}
+
\left\{  
	\begin{tabular}{c}
 	  	$-\dot{\rho} \; \hcos \theta$ \\ 
 		$-\dot{\chi}$ \\ 
 		$-\dot{\rho} \; \hsin \theta$
 	\end{tabular}
\right\}
+
\left\{  
	\begin{tabular}{c}
 	  	$-\rho \hsin \theta \; 
			\dot{\theta} + \chi \; \dot{\psi}$ \\ 
 		$-\rho \hcos \theta \; 
			\dot{\psi} \;\;\;\;\;\;\;\; \;$ \\ 
 		$-\rho \hcos \theta \; 
			\dot{\theta} \;\;\;\;\;\;\;\; \;$
 	\end{tabular}
\right\}
\ee
